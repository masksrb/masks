title: Manage
description: Where everything lives in /manage, and what each page and tab holds.
---

`/manage` is where managers run a tenant. From it they look after:

- actors, the people and machines that sign in
- clients, the apps that use masks to sign actors in
- organizations, providers, and the tenant's settings

You sign in to `/manage` through masks, like any other app. An actor who holds `masks:manage` or one
of the three narrower [manage roles](/guides/security/#manage-roles) can open it. A manager with a
narrower role sees the same pages, and a line at the top of each page says what they can change.

Everything `/manage` does goes through the [manage API](/reference/manage/), so a script can do the
same things.

## Finding your way

The bar at the top holds **Overview**, **Actors**, **Organizations**, and **Clients**, and a button
that opens the settings. On a phone, the four sections move to a bar at the bottom of the screen.

The **Actors** item shows a count of the actors waiting for approval or asking for help.

Every page and tab has its own address:

- `/manage/clients/abc/access` opens the **Access** tab of the client `abc`.
- `/manage/actors?lens=waiting` opens the actors list with the **Awaiting approval** filter on.
- If you open one of these links while signed out, masks signs you in and then opens the link. In a
  browser that has never opened `/manage`, you land on the overview.

## Overview

The overview starts with anything that needs a manager:

- Actors waiting for approval. The link opens that filter on **Actors**.
- Actors who [asked for help](/guides/features/#second-factors) signing in.
- Events from the last day that are worth a look, such as a refused sign-in, a replayed refresh token,
  or a blocked device. It names the latest one and links to **Activity**.

Until each is done, the overview also shows these steps:

1. **Let masks send email**, while the tenant has no mail service.
2. **Register your first client**, while it has no clients.
3. **Invite someone**, while it has one actor or none.

**Hide** puts a step away in this browser only.

Below that, the overview shows:

- how many actors, clients, organizations, and devices the tenant has
- a chart of sign-ins per day over 7, 30, or 90 days
- the first few organizations and their members
- the latest activity

## Actors

The actors list marks each actor who:

- holds a scope that starts with `masks:`
- uses an authenticator app, with the number of backup codes left
- is invited and has not signed in yet
- has an email address they have not confirmed
- is waiting for approval
- is asking for help
- is suspended
- has a blocked device

Filter the list with **Everyone**, **Invited**, **Awaiting approval**, **Asking for help**,
**Managers**, and **Suspended**.

- **Add actor** adds one actor.
- **Invite many** invites several actors by email address at once, and can put them in groups.

The same page lists **Devices**. Filter them with **Allowed**, **Blocked**, and **Never signed in**.
Each device has a page of its own, with its activity. See
[sessions and devices](/guides/features/#sessions-and-devices).

### An actor's page

When something about an actor needs a manager, the top of their page says so and holds the button
that settles it:

- an approval or a request for help
- a suspension, with **Restore**
- an invitation not yet accepted, with **Resend invitation**
- an email address not yet confirmed, with **Send a confirmation link**

| Tab          | What it holds                                                                                                                            |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Profile**  | Their nickname, name, email address, and other profile details. Their photo. **Suspend or delete**.                                      |
| **Access**   | Their scopes and [groups](/guides/features/#groups). The IDs a directory gave them. The apps they have let in. Their connected accounts. |
| **Sign-in**  | Their password, second factors, and passkeys. Where they are signed in. Their tokens.                                                    |
| **Activity** | Their latest 25 events, with a link to **All activity**.                                                                                 |

## Organizations

The organizations list shows each organization's members, open invitations, roles, and how its
members sign in. It marks an organization that has no owner. Archived organizations are in a section
of their own, with **Restore**.

To add one, choose **Add organization**. An organization's page has five tabs: **Members**,
**Sign-in**, **Roles**, **Activity**, and **General**. See
[organizations in /manage](/guides/organizations/#organizations-in-manage) for what each tab holds.

## Clients

The clients list marks a client that registered itself, and says whether a manager has approved it.
Archived clients show only when the **Archived** switch is on.

To add a client, choose **Add client**. Pick the kind of client:

- **Signs in as itself**
- **Signs actors in**
- **Signs actors in over SAML**
- **A known product**, one of the [known products](/guides/features/#adding-a-known-app)

### A client's page

| Tab           | What it holds                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **General**   | Its name and how it proves who it is. Its SAML settings, for a SAML client. What actors see about it, such as its logo and home page. **Sign-in**: its sign-in policy, whether it must push or sign its requests, DPoP, consent, its token and consent lifetimes, and its back-channel logout URI. Archiving it. |
| **Access**    | [Who may sign in](/guides/features/#who-may-sign-in-to-an-app), by group or organization role. The scopes it always gets, and the scopes it may ask for.                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Endpoints** | Its redirect URIs, post-logout redirect URIs, and resources. Its keys. Its [authorization details](/guides/security/#rich-authorization-requests).                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Usage**     | Who has let it in. Its live tokens. Its activity. The namespaces it claimed.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |

## Settings

| Tab           | What it holds                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **General**   | The tenant's name and branding. How long activity is kept. Dynamic registration. Signing keys. [Groups](/guides/features/#groups), domains, and [namespaces](/guides/core-concepts/#namespaces).                      |
| **Policies**  | The [sign-in policies](/guides/features/#sign-in-policies), archived policies, and rules for the whole tenant: the primary identifier, the default policy, **Who may sign in**, and **Auto-deletion** of idle actors. |
| **Providers** | The providers and MCP servers that actors sign in or connect with.                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Directory** | The [LDAP](/guides/ldap/) server. [Provisioning](/guides/enterprise/) tokens.                                                                                                                                         |
| **Messages**  | **Sending** holds the services that send email and text messages. **Templates** holds the wording of each email.                                                                                                      |
| **Activity**  | **Log** holds the tenant's whole activity, which you can filter by kind of event, narrow to what is worth a look, and export. **Streams** holds the [event streams](/guides/event-streams/).                          |

The settings tabs also show who is signed in, with two buttons:

- **Sign out** signs you out of masks in this browser.
- **Forget client** drops this browser's manage client and asks you to approve a new one. The old
  client stays under **Clients** until someone archives it.

### A provider's page

The providers list shows each provider's protocol. It marks a provider that:

- adds the actors it signs in
- trusts the email addresses it confirms
- accepts its [security events](/guides/shared-signals/)
- lets clients use an actor's account there
- has no client secret set

Archived providers are in a section of their own, with **Restore**.

To add a provider, choose **Add provider**. Start from a known provider, such as Google, GitHub, or
Okta, or from a blank OpenID Connect, OAuth 2.0, SAML 2.0, or MCP provider.

| Tab                    | What it holds                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Setup**              | Its name. The values to give the provider. Its endpoints and credentials. Archiving it.                                                                                                            |
| **Sign-in**            | The email domains it signs in. Whether it adds the actors it signs in, and the scopes a new actor gets. Whether it trusts their email addresses. Its security events. Where each claim comes from. |
| **Clients**            | [Delegation](/guides/connecting-via-sdk/#set-up-the-provider): whether clients may use an actor's account there, and what they may do with it.                                                     |
| **Connected accounts** | Every actor who has connected an account through it.                                                                                                                                               |

What each kind of provider shows:

- An MCP server has no **Sign-in** tab. Its **Setup** tab has **Register again**.
- A SAML provider has no **Clients** tab, and no setting to trust email addresses.
- Only an OpenID Connect provider has security events.
- Only a blank provider shows where each claim comes from.

Your changes on a provider's page wait in a bar at the bottom of the screen. Choose **Save** to keep
them, or **Discard** to drop them.

## Activity

Each event reads as a short label, such as _Signed in_ or _Sign-in refused_. It shows who did it,
which client it was for, and when.

- On the overview, and on an actor's or organization's page, the same event repeated in a row shows
  as one line with a count.
- Hover over an event's details to see them in full.
