title: Manage
description: Where everything lives in /manage, and what each page and tab holds.
---

`/manage` is where you run a tenant. From it you look after:

- actors, the people and machines that sign in
- clients, the apps that use masks to sign actors in
- organizations, providers, and the tenant's settings

You sign in to `/manage` through masks, like any other app. Only an actor with a `masks:manage`
scope can open it. Everything `/manage` does goes through the [manage API](/reference/manage/),
so a script can do the same things.

## Who can do what

An actor with `masks:manage` can do everything. Three narrower
[manage roles](/guides/security/#manage-roles) see the same pages but can change less. `/manage`
says at the top of each page what they cannot change.

| Role | What it can do |
| --- | --- |
| `masks:manage:read` | Read everything. Change nothing. |
| `masks:manage:support` | Help actors. Settings, keys, and other managers need an owner. |
| `masks:manage:security` | Change keys, clients, providers, and policies. Actors need support or an owner. |

## Finding your way

The bar at the top holds **Overview**, **Actors**, **Organizations**, and **Clients**. The settings
open from the same bar and have their own tabs. On a phone, the bar is at the bottom of the screen.

Every page and tab has its own address:

- `/manage/clients/abc/access` opens the **Access** tab of the client `abc`.
- `/manage/actors?lens=waiting` opens the actors list with the **Awaiting approval** filter on.
- If you open one of these links while signed out, masks signs you in and then opens the link.

## Overview

The overview starts with anything that needs a manager:

- Actors waiting for approval. The link opens that filter on **Actors**.
- Actors who [asked for help](/guides/features/#second-factors) signing in.
- Events from the last day that are worth a look, such as a refused sign-in, a replayed refresh token,
  or a blocked device. It names the latest one and links to **Activity**.

A new tenant also sees **Getting started**, with three steps:

1. Add a mail service.
2. Register your first client.
3. Invite someone.

A step goes away once it is done. **Hide** puts a step away in this browser only.

Below that, the overview shows:

- how many actors, clients, organizations, and devices the tenant has
- a chart of sign-ins per day
- the first few organizations and their members
- the latest activity

## Actors

The actors list marks each actor who:

- is a manager
- has a second factor
- is invited and has not signed in yet
- has an email address they have not confirmed
- is waiting for approval
- is asking for help
- is suspended
- has a blocked device

Filter the list with **Everyone**, **Invited**, **Awaiting approval**, **Asking for help**,
**Managers**, and **Suspended**. To invite several actors at once, choose **Invite many actors** and
enter their email addresses.

The same page lists **Devices**. Filter them with **Allowed**, **Blocked**, and **Never signed in**.
See [sessions and devices](/guides/features/#sessions-and-devices).

### An actor's page

If something about an actor is waiting on a manager, such as an approval or a request for help, the
top of their page says so. The button that settles it is right there.

| Tab | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Profile** | Their nickname, name, email address, and other profile details. Their photo. **Suspend or delete**. |
| **Access** | Their scopes and [groups](/guides/features/#groups). The IDs a directory gave them. The apps they have let in. Their connected accounts. |
| **Sign-in** | Their password, second factors, and passkeys. Where they are signed in. Their tokens. |
| **Activity** | Everything they did, and everything done to them. |

## Organizations

The organizations list shows each organization's members, owners, and open invitations. It marks an
organization that has no owner.

An organization's page has five tabs: **Members**, **Sign-in**, **Roles**, **Activity**, and
**General**. See [organizations in /manage](/guides/organizations/#organizations-in-manage) for what
each tab holds.

## Clients

The clients list marks a client that registered itself, and says whether a manager has approved it.
It also marks an archived client.

To add a client, choose **Add client**. You can register it by hand, or pick
[a known product](/guides/features/#adding-a-known-app).

### A client's page

| Tab | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **General** | Its name and how it proves who it is. Its SAML settings, for a SAML application. What actors see about it, such as its home page. How long its tokens last. Archiving it. |
| **Access** | [Who may sign in](/guides/features/#who-may-sign-in-to-an-app). The scopes it may ask for. |
| **Endpoints** | Its redirect and logout URIs. Its keys. Its [authorization details](/guides/security/#rich-authorization-requests). |
| **Usage** | Who has let it in. Its live tokens. Its activity. The namespaces it claimed. |

## Settings

| Tab | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **General** | The tenant's name and branding. How long activity is kept. Dynamic registration. Signing keys. [Groups](/guides/features/#groups), domains, and [namespaces](/guides/core-concepts/#namespaces). |
| **Policies** | The [sign-in policies](/guides/features/#sign-in-policies), and which one is the default. |
| **Providers** | The identity providers and MCP servers that actors sign in or connect with. |
| **Directory** | The [LDAP](/guides/ldap/) server. [Provisioning](/guides/enterprise/) tokens. |
| **Messages** | **Sending** holds the services that send email and text messages. **Templates** holds the wording of each message. |
| **Activity** | The tenant's whole activity log, which you can filter by kind of event and export. **Streams** holds the [event streams](/guides/event-streams/). |

The settings tabs also show who is signed in, with two buttons:

- **Sign out** signs you out of `/manage`.
- **Forget client** drops this browser's manage client and asks you to approve a new one. The old
  client stays under **Clients** until someone archives it.

### A provider's page

The providers list shows each provider's protocol. It marks a provider that:

- adds the actors it signs in
- trusts the email addresses it confirms
- accepts its [security events](/guides/shared-signals/)
- lets clients use an actor's account there
- has no client secret set

To add a provider, choose **Add provider**. Start from a preset, such as Google or Microsoft, or from
a blank OpenID Connect, OAuth 2.0, SAML 2.0, or MCP provider.

| Tab | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Setup** | Its name. The values to give the identity provider. Its endpoints and credentials. Archiving it. |
| **Sign-in** | The email domains it signs in. The scopes a new actor gets. Whether it trusts their email addresses. Its security events. Where each claim comes from. |
| **Clients** | [Delegation](/guides/connecting-via-sdk/#set-up-the-provider): whether clients may use an actor's account there, and what they may do with it. |
| **Connected accounts** | Every actor who has connected an account through it. |

An MCP server has no **Sign-in** tab. A SAML provider has no **Clients** tab.

Your changes on a provider's page wait in a bar at the bottom of the screen. Choose **Save** to keep
them, or **Discard** to drop them.

## Activity

Each event reads as a short sentence, such as _Signed in_ or _Token refused_. It shows who did it,
which client it was for, and when.

- On the overview, and on an actor's or organization's page, the same event repeated in a row shows
  as one line with a count.
- Hover over an event's details to see them in full.
