description: Signing in to masks with another service, signing SAML applications in with masks, and provisioning people over SCIM.

masks works with other sign-in services in three ways. Single sign-on (SSO) means an actor signs in
once and uses that sign-in for many apps.

| Direction | masks is | See |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sign in to masks with Google, Okta, Entra, or a SAML identity provider | the service provider, the side that accepts the sign-in | [Providers](#providers) |
| Sign in to a SAML-only app, such as a wiki, with masks | the identity provider, the side that signs actors in | [SAML applications](#saml-applications) |
| Keep masks' actors in sync with a directory | the SCIM service, the side a directory sends changes to | [Provisioning](#provisioning) |

## Providers

A provider is another service that actors sign in with. It uses OpenID Connect, OAuth 2.0, or
SAML 2.0. masks links the account there to an account in masks. That link is a
**connected account**.

A provider takes the place of the password. An actor's
[sign-in policy](/guides/features/#sign-in-policies) still asks for a second factor when the policy
requires one.

### Provider roles

A provider's role decides whether it can add accounts and change profiles.

| Role | Creates accounts | Updates the profile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `credential` | No. It signs in only the account it is connected to. | No. |
| `delegate` | Yes, for a new actor. | Yes. Name, photo, and email address, on every sign-in. |

- Use `delegate` when the company directory decides who works there.
- A `credential` provider may confirm the email address of an account that already exists. It still
  connects to that account only after the actor signs in another way.

### Trusted email addresses

masks tells apps `email_verified: true`, which says the email address is confirmed. So masks trusts
an email address from a provider only when one of these is true:

- The email address is in one of the provider's **email domains**.
- The provider has `trusts_email` on, and the provider reports the email address as confirmed.

Turn on `trusts_email` only for a provider that confirms email addresses. The Google, Apple, GitHub,
GitLab, Slack, LinkedIn, and Discord presets turn it on.

Edge cases:

- An email address that the provider reports as unconfirmed stays unconfirmed, even in a listed
  domain.
- SAML has no field for confirmation. A SAML provider is trusted only for its listed domains.
- For an untrusted email address, a `delegate` provider creates no account, and no invitation is
  accepted.
- An actor with no email address at all still gets an account, with no email address.

### Where a provider is offered

The client's [sign-in policy](/guides/features/#sign-in-policies) decides where a provider is
offered:

- `first_factors` must include `provider`.
- `providers` lists which providers. When it is unset, all providers are offered.

A signed-in actor can connect any offered provider from their account page. They must have signed in
within the last fifteen minutes. masks refuses an account at the provider that is already connected
to someone else.

### Sending a domain to its provider

An actor who types an email address at a company's domain can go straight to that company's
provider. They see no password prompt. masks does this only for a domain the tenant has proven it
controls.

1. In `/manage`, open **Settings**, then **General**.
2. Enter the domain under **Domains**.
3. Choose **Claim**.
4. Publish the TXT record masks shows. A TXT record is a text entry in the domain's DNS settings.
   For example, `_masks-challenge.acme.example` with the value `masks-verification=…`.
5. Choose **Check**, or wait for the hourly check.
6. Once the domain is proven, choose the provider beside it.

After that, an email address at the domain starts the provider's sign-in. This happens even when the
provider belongs to an organization the app did not name. An email address at any other domain, and
a nickname, sign in the usual way.

- Only one tenant at a time can prove a domain. Another tenant's claim to it is refused.
- A proven record that stops answering is released after seven days. Actors then sign in the usual
  way again.
- A sign-in policy without the `provider` factor sends nobody to a provider.

## Provider protocols

### OpenID Connect

masks checks the `id_token` from the provider:

- Its signature, against the issuer's keys.
- Its issuer, audience, and nonce, against the request.

The request uses PKCE, a one-time secret that stops someone else from using the sign-in code. The
subject, the ID for the account at the provider, is `sub`. For a Microsoft directory, it is `oid`.

### OAuth 2.0

When there is no `id_token`, masks reads the provider's userinfo endpoint.

- `subject_claim` and `claims` map each claim to a path in the response. For example, `data.id` for
  X, or `id` for GitHub.
- The request uses PKCE.
- masks discards the provider's tokens once it has the profile.

An **emails URL** lists confirmed email addresses apart from the profile, as GitHub does. masks uses
the primary confirmed email address from it. It ignores an email address that appears only in the
public profile.

### SAML 2.0

1. Open the provider's **Setup** tab in `/manage`.
2. Give the identity provider these two values:

   | | |
   | --- | --- |
   | Assertion consumer service URL | `https://<tenant>/login/provider/<key>/callback` |
   | Service provider entity ID | `https://<tenant>/login/provider/<key>/metadata`, which also serves masks' metadata |

3. Give masks the identity provider's metadata, as a URL or pasted in.

masks fetches a metadata URL again every night, so it picks up a rotated certificate.

An assertion is the signed message in which the identity provider says who signed in. masks trusts
an assertion only when all of these are true:

- It is signed by a certificate in the identity provider's metadata.
- It answers the request this browser sent (`InResponseTo`).
- It names this service provider as audience and recipient.
- It names the identity provider's entity ID as issuer.
- It is within its validity window, with one minute allowed for clock drift.

masks refuses responses that it did not ask for, such as ones the identity provider starts. It marks
each request used, so a response cannot be replayed.

Ask the identity provider for a **persistent** name ID, the value that names the account. A transient
name ID changes on every sign-in, so each sign-in looks like a new actor.

### Apple

Apple's client secret is a short-lived ES256 token. masks signs it with the provider's `.p8` private
key. So the provider stores a team ID, a key ID, and that key.

Apple sends the actor's name only on their first sign-in. masks keeps it.

### Posted callbacks

Apple and SAML post back across sites. Browsers do not send a `SameSite=Lax` cookie with a
cross-site POST. So masks:

1. Holds the posted parameters for five minutes under a one-time handle.
2. Redirects to the same URL as a GET, which sends the cookie.

## SAML applications

masks is the identity provider for apps that sign actors in only over SAML 2.0.

1. The app sends an `AuthnRequest`.
2. masks signs the actor in under their [sign-in policy](/guides/features/#sign-in-policies).
3. masks posts a signed assertion back to the app.

### What to give the application

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Metadata | `https://demo.masks.example/saml/metadata` |
| Entity ID | The same URL. |
| Single sign-on | `https://demo.masks.example/saml/sso`, HTTP-Redirect or HTTP-POST. |
| Single logout | `https://demo.masks.example/saml/slo`, HTTP-Redirect or HTTP-POST. |
| Certificate | In the metadata. One per tenant signing key. |

Most apps need only the metadata URL. After a
[key rotation](/guides/security/#rotating-signing-keys), the app must read the metadata again. The
retiring key's certificate stays in the metadata during the overlap.

### Registering one

1. In `/manage`, open **Clients**.
2. Choose **Add client**.
3. Choose **Signs actors in over SAML**.
4. Paste the app's metadata.
5. Choose **Read it**. masks fills in the app's entity ID, HTTP-POST assertion consumer services,
   request-signing certificate, and HTTP-POST single logout URL.

A SAML app is an approved client with no grants and no secret. It cannot use `/authorize` or
`/token`.

| Setting | |
| --- | --- |
| Entity ID | The `Issuer` its `AuthnRequest` names. |
| Assertion consumer services | The only URLs masks posts to. |
| Names actors by | A persistent identifier (pairwise if the client is), or their email address. |
| Refuse requests it did not sign | Check every request against the app's certificate. |
| Let actors start from masks | Answer `/saml/initiate/<client_id>` without a request. |
| Single logout URL | Where masks posts its logout messages. Without one, the app takes no part in [single logout](#single-logout). |

### What is checked

masks shows an error and posts nothing when a request:

- names an `Issuer` with no registered app
- has an `AssertionConsumerServiceURL` that is not registered, or a binding other than HTTP-POST
- asks for a NameID format masks does not issue
- is more than eight minutes old, is more than three minutes in the future, or has another
  `Destination`
- was already received, or repeats an `ID`
- fails a required signature check (SHA-256 or stronger), or carries two signatures
- repeats a parameter, or is larger than 64 kilobytes
- declares a document type, which is how XML external entities get in

| Request or outcome | Response |
| --- | --- |
| `ForceAuthn` | The actor signs in again. |
| `IsPassive` | `NoPassive`, with no page shown. |
| Suspended, declined, or stopped by the policy | `RequestDenied`, in place of an assertion. |

### What is asserted

The response and the assertion are both signed with RSA-SHA256. The assertion:

- is addressed to the app's entity ID
- is confirmed for the app's assertion consumer service and the request `ID`
- is valid for five minutes
- names the session in `SessionIndex`
- names `https://refeds.org/profile/mfa` when a second factor was used

Attributes use the claim names `email`, `name`, `given_name`, `family_name`, and
`preferred_username`. `samlAttributes` maps them to an app's own names.

- masks releases only what the actor's `openid profile email` consent covers.
- masks releases an email address only once it is confirmed, as an attribute or as the NameID.
- The posting page's content security policy allows only its own script.

### Single logout

Single logout means that signing out in one place signs the actor out of every app the session
used. An app with a single logout URL takes part.

- masks posts every logout message to that URL over HTTP-POST, signed with RSA-SHA256.
- masks records each app a session signed in to, with the NameID the app was given.
- Every assertion names the session in `SessionIndex`.

#### When the app signs a person out

The app sends a `LogoutRequest` to `/saml/slo` over HTTP-Redirect or HTTP-POST. The SAML profiles
require it to be signed on both bindings. masks shows an error and ends nothing when:

- the app has no single logout URL or no certificate
- the request is unsigned, or its signature does not verify against the app's certificate with
  SHA-256 or stronger
- the request fails the age, `Destination`, replay, size, or document type checks an
  `AuthnRequest` gets, or its `NotOnOrAfter` has passed
- the request names no `NameID`

Otherwise, masks ends every session in which the app was given that NameID.

- A request that carries a `SessionIndex` ends only the sessions it names.
- When no session matches, masks ends nothing and answers `Requester` with `UnknownPrincipal`.
- Otherwise it answers `Success`.
- Either answer is a signed `LogoutResponse`, posted to the single logout URL with the request's
  `RelayState`.

An ended session ends as it does at `/logout`. OpenID Connect clients get their
[back-channel logout](/guides/features/#telling-other-apps) tokens. The session's other SAML apps
are signed out as the next section describes.

#### When a person signs out of masks

Each app with a single logout URL that the session signed in to gets one `LogoutRequest`. The
request names the NameID and `SessionIndex` from that app's assertion.

| How the session ends | How the apps hear |
| --- | --- |
| `/logout`, which **Sign out** uses | The browser carries a `LogoutRequest` to each app in turn, and brings back its `LogoutResponse`. After the last app, the browser goes where the logout was going. |
| An app's `LogoutRequest`, sent from the browser holding the session | The browser visits the other apps the same way. Then it carries masks' answer to the app that asked. |
| Any other way. A manager signs the actor out, a device is signed out, the actor is suspended, the session times out, or an app sends a `LogoutRequest` without the session's cookie. | masks posts the `LogoutRequest` to each app from the server, as a `SAMLRequest` form field. A `2xx` or `3xx` answer counts as delivered. masks tries up to five times. A delivery that still fails is recorded as an event. |

masks reads a `LogoutResponse` from an app only when it answers a request masks sent that app in the
last ten minutes. masks checks it against the app's certificate when the app has one.

A delivery from the server reaches an app without the actor's browser. So it ends only sessions the
app can find by NameID or `SessionIndex`.

masks does not support:

- encrypted assertions
- the artifact and SOAP bindings
- choosing an assertion consumer service by index

## Provisioning

Provisioning means a directory adds, changes, and removes accounts in masks for you. A directory
such as Entra ID, Okta, Google Workspace, or JumpCloud keeps masks' actors in sync over SCIM 2.0
([RFC 7643](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7643),
[RFC 7644](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7644)). It adds, renames, and suspends actors in masks
as it does in its own directory.

Each tenant's base URL is its issuer followed by `/scim/v2`:

```
https://demo.masks.example/scim/v2
```

### Connecting a directory

1. In `/manage`, open **Settings**, then **Directory**.
2. Issue a token under **Provisioning**.
3. Label it after the directory.
4. Choose a lifetime, up to one year.
5. Copy the token. masks shows it once.
6. In the directory, enter the base URL, and enter the token as a bearer token. A bearer token is a
   secret that grants access to whoever sends it.

The token list shows each token's last use. A revoked token is refused.

A client can also provision. The client must:

- be approved and allowed `masks:scim`
- request its own token with [`client_credentials`](/guides/features/#signing-in-as-itself)
- name the base URL as its `resource`

### What is provisioned

`/Users` supports reads, filters, creation, `PUT`, `PATCH`, and deletion.

| SCIM | masks |
| --- | --- |
| `id` | The actor's `uuid`. |
| `userName` | The nickname, or the email address when it is an email address. |
| `externalId` | Stored and filterable. It is unique in the tenant, or in the organization for a [directory for one organization](#a-directory-for-one-organization). |
| `name`, `displayName` | The name claims. |
| `emails` | The primary email address, treated as confirmed. |
| `phoneNumbers`, `photos`, `profileUrl`, `locale`, `timezone` | The matching claims. |
| `active` | Whether the actor is suspended. |
| `password` | Set, if the directory sends one. |

A provisioned actor holds the standard scopes. They have no password unless the directory sent one.
They sign in in one of two ways:

- through a [provider](#providers) that trusts the same email address
- by accepting an invitation

| Filters | |
| --- | --- |
| Operators | `eq`, `ne`, `co`, `sw`, `ew`, `pr`, `gt`, `ge`, `lt`, `le` |
| Joins | Up to four comparisons with `and`. |
| Attributes | `userName`, `externalId`, `emails.value`, `displayName`, `name.givenName`, `name.familyName`, `active`, the `meta` dates |
| `eq` only | `userName`, `active` |
| Case | `externalId` compares exactly. The other attributes ignore case. |

- Every response has a weak `ETag`, a tag for the version of the record. An `If-Match` naming an
  older version is refused.
- `/ServiceProviderConfig`, `/ResourceTypes`, and `/Schemas` describe the rest.
- `/Groups` answers only a [directory for one organization](#roles-as-groups).
- Bulk operations and sorting are not supported.

### Suspending

`active: false`, or **Suspend** in `/manage`, suspends an actor.

- Every session ends.
- Every token stops working, refresh tokens included.
- Sign-in is refused after the first factor. So entering a name alone does not reveal the
  suspension.

`active: true` restores the actor. `DELETE` removes them.

### What a directory cannot do

A provisioning token changes many accounts. So it cannot:

- set the password or email address of anyone holding a `masks:` scope
- suspend or delete the last active holder of `masks:manage`
- change anyone's scopes

Every change is recorded in the actor's activity, marked as SCIM.

### A directory for one organization

A provisioning token can belong to one [organization](/guides/organizations/). Then a customer's
directory manages that customer's actors and nobody else.

Only an owner can issue a token for every account, or let a client carry `masks:scim`. Either one
reaches managers too.

| Request | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| List or read users | Only the organization's members. Anyone else is not found. |
| Create a user | A new account that joins as `member`. Its email address must be at a domain proven for one of the organization's providers, and is then treated as confirmed. Any other email address is refused with `400` before masks looks for an account holding it. A user without an email address is allowed. |
| Replace or patch a user | Allowed only for an account this directory created that belongs to no other organization and manages nothing. Anyone else is refused with `403`. So an owner who adds an existing account cannot then take it over through the directory. |
| Delete a user | Removes them from the organization and revokes its tokens. The account stays. |

The organization's directory keeps its own `externalId` for each member. So two organizations'
directories can use the same value.

A `userName`, email address, or `externalId` that is already taken is refused with `409`. The answer
has the same `uniqueness` detail every time, so it does not say who holds the value or where.

An actor's **Access** tab in `/manage` shows the `externalId` each organization's directory holds
for them.

### Roles as groups

A directory for one organization manages roles through `/Groups`. Each of the organization's roles
is one group.

- Its `displayName` is the role.
- Its `id` is the organization's uuid and the role, joined by a dot.
- Its members are the actors who have accepted that role.

| Request | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| List or read groups | Every role, `owner` and `member` included. Filter with `displayName eq` or `id eq`. `excludedAttributes=members` leaves members out. |
| Add a member | Gives them that role in place of the one they held. |
| Remove a member | Moves them back to `member`. Removing from `member` changes nothing. They stay in the organization. |
| Replace members with `PUT` or a `replace` operation | Adds everyone named and moves everyone else back to `member`. Actors this directory did not create keep their role. For `member`, it only adds. |
| Create a group | Adds a role with that name. An existing role is a conflict. |
| Delete a group | Removes the role once nobody holds it. `owner` and `member` cannot be deleted. |
| Rename a group | Refused. |

A group takes only actors this directory created who belong to no other organization and manage
nothing.

- Naming anyone else is refused with `400`. The answer is the same whether or not the account
  exists.
- Removing the last owner is refused with `409`.
- A role change revokes the actor's access tokens for the organization. It is recorded in the
  organization's activity, marked as SCIM.

A token for every account lists no groups and cannot change them.
