This page lists what a company needs to audit, control, and prove how people sign in. Each section
links to the guide that covers it, except step-up authentication, which this page covers.

## Activity

masks records an event for each sign-in, consent, change a manager makes, revoked token, and signing
key rotation. Each event names the action, the actor, who did it, the client, the device, the IP
address, and the user agent.

In `/manage`, **Settings**, then **Activity**, lists every event. Filter it by kind of event, by
actor, or by client, or show **Only what is worth a look**, such as a refused sign-in.

- [Keeping and exporting activity](/guides/security/#keeping-and-exporting-activity): keep events
  from 30 days to seven years, and download a range as a file.
- [Event streams](/guides/event-streams/): post each event, signed, to an HTTPS endpoint as it
  happens.

## Shared signals

masks sends and receives security events through the OpenID Shared Signals Framework.

- As a transmitter, masks pushes signed events to a receiver when a session is revoked, a credential
  changes, or an account is suspended. See [shared signals](/guides/shared-signals/).
- As a receiver, masks signs an actor out when their provider says a session was revoked or the
  account was disabled. See
  [receiving signals from a provider](/guides/shared-signals/#receiving-signals-from-a-provider).

## Single sign-on and provisioning

- [Providers](/guides/sso-saml/#providers): sign actors in through OpenID Connect, OAuth 2.0, and
  SAML 2.0 providers.
- [Sending a domain to its provider](/guides/sso-saml/#sending-a-domain-to-its-provider): send
  someone who types an email address at a proven domain straight to their company's provider.
- [SAML applications](/guides/sso-saml/#saml-applications): sign actors in to apps that speak only
  SAML.
- [Provisioning](/guides/sso-saml/#provisioning): keep actors in step with a directory over SCIM.

## Custom domains

A tenant can serve sign-in from its own host, such as `login.example.com`, as a second issuer beside
its usual address. See [custom domains](/guides/self-hosting/#custom-domains).

## Organizations

An organization is a customer inside a tenant. Its members each hold a role, and an app that asks
for the `organization` scope gets it in an `org` claim. An organization can have its own sign-in
policy, provider, directory, and event stream. See [organizations](/guides/organizations/).

## Manage roles

`masks:manage:read`, `masks:manage:support`, and `masks:manage:security` let an auditor, a support
team, and a security team each do their job in `/manage` and nothing more. See
[manage roles](/guides/security/#manage-roles).

## Sign-in policies

A [sign-in policy](/guides/features/#sign-in-policies) sets:

- [how long a session lasts](/guides/features/#how-long-a-session-lasts), and when an idle one ends
- [an emailed code](/guides/features/#signing-in-with-an-emailed-code) as a first factor, with or
  without a password
- the risk score that asks for a second factor or refuses a
  [risky sign-in](/guides/security/#risky-sign-ins)
- whether a second factor is required

## Step-up authentication

Some actions need a stronger or fresher sign-in than the session holds, such as moving money.

ID tokens carry `auth_time`, `amr`, and `acr`. The `acr` is the level the sign-in reached:
`urn:masks:acr:mfa` with a second factor, and `urn:masks:acr:pwd` without one.

A client asks for more on `/authorize`:

- `max_age` or `prompt=login` asks for a fresh sign-in.
- `acr_values=urn:masks:acr:mfa` asks for a second factor. So does asking for the `acr` claim with
  only that value in the `claims` parameter.

When a second factor is asked for:

- A session that already used one is not asked again.
- Otherwise masks asks for a second factor the actor holds, or asks them to add one.
- `acr_values` lists the values the client accepts. A request that also accepts `urn:masks:acr:pwd`
  is satisfied by a password.

Two settings under **Second factor** in a sign-in policy do the same for every sign-in:

- **Required for everyone, not just managers** makes every actor hold a second factor.
- **Asked again at a sign-in to a client when the session did not use one** asks for a second factor
  before the client receives a code, even when the session started with a password alone.

## Token exchange

A service acting for a person, including an AI agent, can trade the token it holds for a narrower
one to use with a downstream service (RFC 8693). Each exchange is recorded as an event, and a
[rich authorization request](/guides/security/#rich-authorization-requests) can limit the new token
to one action, such as one payment to one payee. See
[exchanging a token](/guides/connecting-via-sdk/#exchanging-a-token).
