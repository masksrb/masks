An **organization** is a customer inside a tenant.

- Its **members** are accounts.
- Each member holds one **role** in the organization.
- Every organization has the roles `owner` and `member`. It can offer more, such as `billing`.
- An account can belong to several organizations, or to none.

| Who | Where | Can |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A manager | `/manage`, under **Organizations** | Add and archive organizations. Set their sign-in. Add, promote, and remove anyone. |
| An owner | Their own account page, under **Organizations** | Invite, promote, and remove the members of their own organization. |
| A member | Their own account page | See their role. Accept or decline an invitation. Leave. |

In `/manage`, each change needs one of the [manage roles](/guides/security/#manage-roles):

- `masks:manage:security` to add, archive, or restore an organization, or to change its roles or
  sign-in.
- `masks:manage:support` to add members, invite them again, change their role, or remove them.

## Adding an organization

1. In `/manage`, open **Organizations** and choose **Add organization**.
2. Enter a name. masks fills in the key from the name. Apps ask for the organization by its key, such
   as `organization=acme`. A key uses lowercase letters, digits, and dashes.
3. Choose **Add organization** again to save it. You become its first owner.
4. Add the customer's own owner as a member in the `owner` role.
5. Once they accept, remove yourself if you want to.

### Roles

A role is a word that the member's tokens carry.

- To add a role, open the organization's **Roles** tab, enter it under **Offer another role**, and
  choose **Add**.
- A role uses lowercase letters, digits, dashes, and underscores. It is up to 40 characters long.
- `owner` and `member` are always offered.
- A role cannot be removed while someone holds it.

## Inviting members

Adding someone to an organization invites them. Until they accept, the invitation gives them nothing:

- no `org` claim in their tokens
- no place in the list of organizations to choose from
- nothing for the organization's directory to change

To invite someone from `/manage`:

1. Open the organization and choose **Add member**.
2. Enter the person's email address.
3. Choose a role.
4. Choose **Invite**.

An owner invites someone from **Organizations** on their own account page. They enter the address
under **Add someone by email**, choose a role, and choose **Add**. An owner needs no `/manage`
access, and reaches only the organizations they own.

masks emails the person. The email names the organization, the role, and who invited them.

| The address belongs to | The email |
| --- | --- |
| An account with a confirmed address | Links to the invitation on their account page. They accept or decline it there. |
| No account | An account invitation to choose a password. Once they have an account, the organization is waiting on their account page. |
| An account with no confirmed address | Nothing is sent. The invitation waits on their account page. |

When neither the tenant nor the server has a way to send mail, `/manage` shows the account
invitation's link so the manager can send it. The link works once.

### Who an owner can invite

A manager can invite any address. An owner can invite an address in these cases:

- The address is at a domain proven for one of the organization's providers.
- The organization's sign-in policy admits the address, and that policy allows signup.

Some details about this rule:

- The organization's sign-in policy is its own policy when it has one. Otherwise it is the tenant's
  policy.
- The rule does not depend on whether an account holds the address. So the answer does not reveal
  who has an account.
- An owner sends at most 50 invitations a day. Invitations sent again count toward this limit.
- A new account made by an invitation gets the policy's signup scopes.

### Accepting

Only an account that holds the address, confirmed, can accept an invitation sent to that address.

- The invitation waits on their account page, under **Waiting for you**. It shows who sent it, the
  role, when it expires, and the address it went to.
- An account whose address is not confirmed is asked to confirm it under **Email address** first.
- An invitation sent to another address says where it went. The person signs in with that address.
  Or they ask an owner to invite the address on this account.
- Signing in through the organization's own [provider](#provider) accepts a waiting invitation
  without these checks.

Declining removes the invitation. An owner has to invite the person again.

### Sending an invitation again

An organization invitation expires two weeks after it was last sent. An owner changes this under
**Links** in `/manage`. See [Link lifetimes](/guides/features/#link-lifetimes).

1. Open the organization's members, in `/manage` or on an owner's account page.
2. Find the person. An open invitation is marked **invited**. An expired one is marked **expired**.
3. Choose **Send again**.

Sending again does three things:

- emails the person
- starts the lifetime over
- records *Organization invitation sent again*

An invitation can be sent again at most once an hour.

An expired invitation cannot be accepted. The nightly sweep removes it and records *Organization
invitation expired*. The sweep waits until the invitation has been expired for as long as it was
open. By default, that is four weeks after it was last sent.

## Changing roles and removing members

| Change | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| A member's role changes | Their access tokens for the organization are revoked. The app's next refresh carries the new role. |
| A member is removed | Every token issued for them in the organization is revoked, refresh tokens included. |
| An invitation is withdrawn | It is removed. Nothing had been granted. |
| A member leaves | The same as being removed. An owner has to invite them again. |

An organization always keeps an owner.

- The only owner cannot leave, step down, or be removed until someone else is an owner.
- Two owners cannot remove each other at the same time.
- When the last owner's account is deleted, masks records *Organization left without an owner*. Only
  managers can change its members until they make someone an owner.

An app that receives [shared signals](/guides/shared-signals/), and holds a token for the
organization, hears about role changes and removals as they happen.

### What members are told

masks emails a member when someone else changes their place in an organization.

| Email | Sent when |
| --- | --- |
| Role changed | Their role changes. The email names the old role and the new one. |
| Removed | They are removed from the organization. |
| Archived | The organization is archived while they are signed in to an app for it. |

- A member who makes the change themselves, such as by leaving, gets no email.
- These emails go only to a confirmed address.
- Each person can turn them off on the account page, under **Emails we send you**, in the
  **Organizations** group. See [security emails](/guides/security/#security-emails).

## Signing in as a member

An app asks for the `organization` scope. Its tokens then carry an `org` claim for one organization.
A claim is a named value inside a token.

```json
{ "org": { "id": "5f0c…", "key": "acme", "name": "Acme", "role": "billing" } }
```

| The person | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Belongs to one organization | masks uses that one, without a question. |
| Belongs to several | masks asks which one, after they sign in. |
| Belongs to none | The app gets `access_denied`. |

### Naming the organization

- An app that already knows the organization passes `organization=acme` on `/authorize`. This skips
  the question.
- A person who is not a member of that organization gets `access_denied`.
- An app can name an organization without asking for the scope. The sign-in is then still limited to
  its members and still follows its sign-in policy.

### Switching and refreshing

- To switch organizations, the app sends the person through `/authorize` again with another key.
  The session carries over. A person already signed in the way the organization requires is not asked
  for a password.
- A refresh reads the member's role again.
- A refresh for someone who is no longer a member is refused with `invalid_grant`.

### Device sign-in

A [device sign-in](/guides/features/#device-sign-in) can name the organization with `organization`
on `/device_authorization`. Or the person chooses one when they approve it.

- Its tokens carry that organization.
- Redeeming the device code is refused once the membership has ended.

## An organization's own sign-in

An organization can have its own sign-in policy, provider, and directory. Set all three on the
organization's **Sign-in** tab in `/manage`.

### Policy

A member signs in under the organization's policy first, ahead of the app's policy and the tenant's.
The policy applies once masks knows the organization. That happens after the first factor, when masks
takes the organization the app named or the person chooses one.

When the app names the organization on `/authorize`, the first step can already offer the
organization's own providers.

Once the policy applies:

- A person may have signed in with a first factor the policy does not list, or through a provider it
  does not offer. They see **That organization asks you to sign in another way.** They sign in again
  with a first factor the policy accepts.
- A person whose address is outside the policy's `email_domains` is refused with `access_denied`.
- The second factor, session lifetime, and step-up follow the policy.

### Provider

A provider handed to an organization is offered only to a request that names the organization.
Everyone who signs in through it becomes a member, in the role their groups map to.

- The provider reads groups from the `groups` claim, or from another claim it names.
- The first mapped group a person holds decides the role.
- A person in no mapped group gets the unmapped role. This is `member` unless you set another.
- The role is set again on every sign-in.
- Sometimes the provider would take away the last owner's role. Then masks records *Organization role
  kept against the provider*, and the role stays.
- An invited account that has no password yet is linked only when masks
  [trusts the address](/guides/sso-saml/#trusted-email-addresses) from the provider. Otherwise the
  sign-in is refused, and the invitation stays waiting.
- An existing account with the same address is linked only after that account signs in another way
  once. This is the same as with any [provider](/guides/sso-saml/#provider-roles).

### Directory

A provisioning token for one organization reaches only that organization's members. It manages their
roles through SCIM groups. See
[a directory for one organization](/guides/sso-saml/#a-directory-for-one-organization).

## What apps read

| Where | `org` | `orgs` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ID token, access token | The organization signed in to. The role held when the token was issued. | |
| Userinfo | The organization the token was issued for. The role held now. | Every organization the person has joined. The role held in each. |
| Introspection | The organization the token was issued for. The role held now. | |

- `org` appears when the token names an organization.
- `orgs` appears when the token holds the `organization` scope.
- Each entry in `orgs` has the same `id`, `key`, `name`, and `role` as `org`.
- `orgs` lists only joined organizations. It leaves out open invitations and archived organizations.
- Discovery lists both claims in `claims_supported`.

```json
{
  "sub": "8f4c…",
  "org": { "id": "5f0c…", "key": "acme", "name": "Acme", "role": "billing" },
  "orgs": [
    { "id": "5f0c…", "key": "acme", "name": "Acme", "role": "billing" },
    { "id": "a21e…", "key": "globex", "name": "Globex", "role": "owner" }
  ]
}
```

### In a Rails app

`Masks::Rails` reads both claims. It limits pages and APIs to members in a role. See
[organizations in a Rails app](/reference/ruby/#organizations).

### In a single-page app

In `@masks/client`, an account carries `organization` and `organizations`. `holdsRole` checks the
role, and `switchOrganization` sends the person through sign-in again, naming another organization.
The `Actor` components for React and Svelte list the other organizations when given
`onSwitchOrganization`. See [@masks/client](/reference/javascript/client/).

## Organizations in /manage

An organization with no owner is marked **no owner**, on the overview and under **Organizations**.

An organization's page counts its members, owners, open invitations, and live tokens. It opens on
**Members**.

| Tab | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Members** | **Everyone**, **Owners**, or **Invited**, which includes expired invitations. Each person's role. **Send again**, and removal. |
| **Sign-in** | The sign-in policy. The organization's providers and their group mapping. Its domains, and whether each is proven. Any provisioning token for it. |
| **Roles** | The roles it offers. How many members hold each. |
| **Activity** | Changes to the organization. What happened while someone signed in as a member. |
| **General** | Its name, key, and ID. The `organization=` value apps ask for it with. Archiving it, with how many live tokens that revokes. |

Archiving an organization does two things:

- revokes every token issued for it
- stops it from being offered

Members keep their roles, so **Restore** puts everything back.

## An organization's activity

An event belongs to an organization in these cases:

- It is about the organization or its members.
- It happens while someone signs in to an app as a member, such as consent or a refused sign-in.
- It is a refresh or exchange of a token issued for the organization. The token carries the
  organization along.

The organization's **Activity** tab lists its recent events. An
[event stream](/guides/event-streams/) can send only those events.
