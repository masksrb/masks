title: What masks is
description: masks is a sign-in server you run yourself. People sign in once, and masks signs them in to your apps.
---

masks is a sign-in server. You run it on a computer of your own, and your apps use it to sign
people in.

## One account for every app

Each person has one account on your masks server. They sign in to masks with a password or a
passkey, and masks signs them in to the apps you have connected. They do not need a separate
password for each app.

An account can hold:

- a password, a passkey, or both
- a second step at sign-in, such as a code from an authenticator app
- backup codes, for the day the phone is lost
- a name, a photo, an email address, and a phone number

Each person has an [account page](/use/your-account/) where they change these themselves.

## Apps you already run

masks knows how to set up many apps that people run at home or at work, such as Grafana,
Nextcloud, Jellyfin, Immich, Home Assistant, and Tailscale. You pick the app from a list, enter
its address, and masks shows you what to paste into the app's settings. See
[connect an app from the known list](/use/connect-an-app/).

An app that has no sign-in of its own can sit behind a web server such as Caddy, Traefik, or
nginx, and masks signs people in to it there. That setup is for operators, and it is covered in
[forward authentication](/build/forward-auth/).

## Who runs it

The first account you create is a manager. A manager opens **Manage this server** from their
account page. There, managers:

- invite people, and approve the ones who sign up on their own
- connect apps, and choose who may sign in to each one
- set the rules for signing in, such as whether a second step is required
- see what has been happening, and check the server's health

A manager can make other people managers too.

## Organizations

An organization is a customer inside your server, such as a company you serve. Each one has its
own members, and each member holds a role there. An app that cares about organizations learns
which one a person signed in for, and their role. See [organizations](/use/organizations/).

## What it needs

masks runs as one container and keeps everything it knows in one place. It needs a way to send
email for invitations, password resets, and sign-in codes. You add a mail service once, in
**Manage this server**.

Sign-in screens, account pages, and the email masks sends are in English, French, Spanish, and
German. masks picks the language the browser asks for.

masks is open source. The code is on [GitHub](https://github.com/masksrb/masks).
