title: Actors
description: An account on a tenant — who signs in, and what they may ask for.
---

An actor is an account. It is named by a nickname, an email address, or both, and carries a list of
scopes that bounds everything it can be granted.

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| `nickname` | typed at sign-in; renameable without a new account |
| `email` / `email_verified_at` | changing the address takes its verification with it |
| `scopes` | the ceiling on what any token for this actor may carry |
| `activated_at` | unset while an invitation is outstanding |

## What names an account

Which half an account must have is one setting, decided once. The first-run screen asks for it, and
`MASKS_NAMED_BY` pins it at deploy instead — a pinned rule wins over the stored one, and changing it
means changing the deployment.

| `MASKS_NAMED_BY` | |
| --- | --- |
| `nickname` | every account needs one; an address is optional |
| `email` | every account needs an address; a nickname is optional |
| `either` | one of the two is enough (the default) |

Whatever the rule, no account can have neither — the database refuses it as well as the model — and
**an actor holding `masks:manage` needs both**. A manager is written to, not just about: invitations,
password resets and address confirmations all go somewhere. Granting `masks:manage` to an account
with only one half is refused until the other is filled in.

Either half signs in: `Actor.locate` takes a nickname or an address, and the sign-in screen asks for
whichever you have. `identifier` answers with the nickname when there is one and the address when
there is not, which is what the console, the audit trail and the introspection endpoint display.

Second factors are a TOTP authenticator, passkeys, and backup codes. A passkey can serve as both
factors at once.

Three predicates decide what an actor may have:

- `holds?(scope)` — is this exact scope in their list
- `withheld(scopes)` — which of these they cannot be granted
- `grant!(scopes)` — add scopes, as claiming a [namespace](/concepts/namespaces/) does

The first actor on a tenant is made by the first-run screen and holds `masks:manage`. That screen is
three tabs — identification, credentials, configuration — asking for the manager's nickname, address
and optional name, then their password twice, then what this installation is called. It never
appears again once the tenant has an actor. After that, accounts are invited or created from the
console, and the console holds every other setting a tenant has.
